# Can I Actually Code? 

## Tutorials, Coding, and The Blank Repo Problem

## My Backstory 

As I wrapped up my summer internship and headed into my first term this year, I decided it was finally time to get cracking on some actual side projects instead of following my usual rhythm of getting lost in an endless stream of Lakers highlights. 

During my internship, I spent a lot of time using React to build components, fix up UIs, refactor hooks, and do all of the other usual stuff you do as a web dev. So naturally, I decided to build a task tracker by following the React Docs, which was simple enough, and then moved on to the Next.js App Router Tutorial. Working through these tutorials was easy enough; on paper, I’d learned all the basics of React and Next.js, but there was a catch…

## The Moment It Cracked

When I finally sat down to initialise and create a new Next.js app, I was brimming with what was ultimately a false sense of confidence as I started to build. This illusion, however, was shattered as soon as I tried to write a simple component, BioCard, for the biography section of my personal site. 

I sat there, having no idea where to start, and in my mind the temptation to give in and use AI to generate the component grew stronger and stronger. I knew I wanted to do this on my own to prove to myself I had the skills to build, but I also felt lost, leading me to question if I actually learned anything from the tutorials I put myself through. 

## Why Does This Happen?

Using tutorials is a lot like riding a bike with training wheels on: they help you get from start to finish, but as soon as they disappear, you are left with a world of problems, and you’ll most likely crash and fall sooner than you think. 

In other words, tutorials teach you how to recognise a problem, but, more often than not, they don’t teach you how to break a problem down and actually generate a solution. That is a genuinely different skill, and tutorials usually avoid this skill to ensure they are easy to follow and friction-free, which is the whole point of a tutorial. 

## What Got Me Unstuck

The solution to my problem of writing the BioCard component, and all other problems down the track where I inevitably got stuck, was something I had already been doing throughout my uni courses that I didn’t even think to use for my projects. It wasn’t sitting through more tutorials, but rather talking myself through the layout out loud in plain English, which made the problem a lot more obvious almost immediately. 

It may seem silly, but if the nitty-gritty details and nuances are too complex to handle inside your head, then sometimes it can be easier to just dumb it down, put it on paper, and build yourself up step by step until you finally get a solution. 

## A Quick Aside On AI

However, this is a lot easier said than done, especially in the age of AI, where terminal-based coding agents can generate code for your codebase faster than you can drop and give me 10 pushups (well, I suppose that depends on your upper body strength, but I digress). Having become so reliant on AI over the last couple of years, sitting with difficult problems and coming to terms with how skilled I actually am without AI is an uncomfortable process to say the least. 

Despite this, I have hope that if I genuinely commit to doing more and more little things without AI, I’ll get 1% better at it each time, and that’s all you need for compounding effects to surprise you. As I said, whilst AI can do a lot of this already, I like to pride myself on actually being able to do this stuff on my own, which is why I want to keep getting 1% better with every problem I solve.

## The Bottom Line

The main takeaway here is that tutorials are great, but if you want to see how well you can actually build anything, try staring at a blank repo and see what you can come up with. As someone who has only just started to build without these coding agents again, I can tell you it will probably be scary. However, it will also get you one step closer to being someone who can actually code up cool stuff. So in my humble opinion, it’ll be worth it in the end. Happy Coding\!
